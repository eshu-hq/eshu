Arbiter ruling (arbiter-eshu, binding), verified at origin/main `49da4cc774`.

#11117 is three defects, not one "unbounded transaction". Keep #11117 for restart-triggered fulls plus the undrained startup full (A+B, one release); split bounding (C) and keyed headroom/detoast (D) into two new issues; do not hold the 49da4cc774 rollout.

1. Mechanisms. M1 real: `fullDone` is a local in `Run` (go/internal/reducer/supplychain/core/winners_maintainer.go); an unclaimed lease or a Skipped resweep returns rebuilt=false (winners_keyed_maintainer.go); the maintainer starts unconditionally at go/internal/reducer/service_side_runners.go (wired at cmd/reducer/main.go); cancel retries with doubled backoff. M2 real: the startup full stamps the pod's Go clock from tick start (winners_keyed_maintainer.go); the rebuild SQL sets materialized_at = $1 unconditionally and never touches the dirty log (supply_chain_impact_canonical_winners_rebuild.go); keyed ticks stamp GREATEST(prev, horizon) so rows captured before the full's stamp pin the watermark until they drain. Correction to the issue body: 01:25:56Z is the general pod's tick-start stamp, committed ~03:12Z; "no resweep committed 01:25-05:13Z" is false (record as AC1). Correction to the lane: the keyed fallback deletes only admitted, version-matching rows (keyed_settle_sql.go) and stamps the horizon when a residual exists (keyed.go); it is not a whole-log drain. New: the audit backstop runs the same full recompute in one statement (supply_chain_impact_winners_audit.go) every 10 min by default (cmd/reducer/winners_wiring.go), on the writer unless stale routing is on (observed_service_wiring.go).
2. Split (existing labels only). #11117 stays, retitled "Stop a reducer roll from re-running the full winners resweep, and drain the dirty log a full covers"; labels bug, backend, capability:reducer-scale, priority:high; AC2 = a restart with a matching contract runs zero fulls and a full leaves no dirty row captured before its stamp; AC3/AC4 stay. New C: "Bound every full winners recompute (rebuild, keyed-cap fallback, writer-backed audit) to short keyset transactions with a persisted cursor" (bug, backend, capability:reducer-scale, reliability; takes the old AC2 snapshot-age RED). New D: "Winners keyed tick has no headroom at QA inflow (~6 keys/s, ~100% duty); payload is re-detoasted per extracted field" (backend, performance, capability:reducer-scale, priority:high).
3. A (skip the startup full). Persist contract_version (integer) and contract_hash on supply_chain_impact_winners_materialization, written only in the transaction that commits a full; both nullable. Hash covers the rendered rebuildSupplyChainImpactWinnersSQL plus every rendered keyed build/apply statement; a hermetic test pins hash to version (text change fails until the author bumps the version or re-pins the hash with a differential proof; a migration rewriting impact facts or scope activation outside the capture owners must bump the version). Decision per tick from the persisted row, replacing the per-process flag: no row/NULL/stored < binary -> full required; stored == binary -> keyed and audit run; stored > binary -> stand down (no full, keyed or audit) and emit a signal. "Skipped, then the other pod commits" is satisfied by re-reading the row. No new leader election (lease + advisory lock are single-flight). Do NOT restrict the maintainer to the claiming-domain deployment. Skip + audit is sufficient only if the audit is proven to complete on QA: that is A's theory gate.
4. B (drain what the full covers). Under the resweep lock, one transaction, this statement order: read S from the DB clock; delete the dirty-log rows and staged rows visible to that delete; then run the rebuild. Marks are staged in the transaction that makes the change (supply_chain_impact_winners_capture.go) so any row the delete sees is visible to the later rebuild snapshot under READ COMMITTED. Delete after rebuild is forbidden. Stamp GREATEST(prev, LEAST(S, min(captured_at) of the residual log and staging minus 1us)); S from the DB clock, not m.now(). Keep the key-ordered FOR UPDATE version-recheck shape. A failed full rolls the deletes back; a mark that executes before S and commits after the rebuild snapshot stays queued for the next tick.
5. C is still needed after A+B (first population, version bump (#11103 and #11113 will cause one), keyed-cap fallback, writer-backed audit all remain single ~6,400-6,600 s statements). Design-first under its own arbiter review. Constraints: batches partition by package_id and each batch deletes stored winners in its range no longer produced; a batch drains only kind='fact' dirty rows in its range, in its own transaction, before its rebuild statement (scope rows left to keyed ticks); keyed ticks do not run and no batch stamps or creates the watermark row until the final batch, which stamps GREATEST(prev, LEAST(S_start, horizon)) and writes the contract version; a statement timeout is a guard, not the fix.
6. D separate issue: yes. Byte-parity rule (supply_chain_impact_canonical_winners_store.go) is met by a derived differential on representative data; re-pin the hash without a version bump only with that proof.
7. Order. PR1 = B, PR2 = A with the AC4 telemetry, deployed in one release (A's NULL-version first full then drains; one roll cycle). Then C, then D. Hazards: migration number/checksum manifest collides with any open lane adding a migration; #11103 and #11113 must bump the contract version if they merge after A (if before, A pins at rebase); #11114 touches the wake call inside ResweepWinners: expect a textual conflict. RED tests A: matching version -> first tick keyed with zero ResweepWinners calls; Skipped N times then a matching row -> zero own fulls; NULL/lower -> exactly one full across two concurrent maintainers on live Postgres; higher -> stand down with no writes; hash gate needs a seeded-violation RED/GREEN pair. RED tests B (live Postgres): seeded pre-S log and staging rows are gone after the full and the next keyed tick advances the watermark; late-commit mark; rollback restores rows. Theory gates: audit duration and last success on QA (A); EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) in BEGIN/ROLLBACK for the ~60k-row drain under 5 s (B); one keyset batch with an index plan, sum of batches <= 1.25x the 6,388-6,635 s baseline, each transaction <= 60 s (C); differential equality plus before/after (D). Measurement on the same QA corpus: before 12,776-13,270 s (3h33m-3h41m) with no keyed commit per roll; after A+B zero path="full" samples after a matching-version roll, first keyed commit within the lease TTL plus two intervals, maintainer transaction age <= 5 min sampled every 30 s for 1 h. AC4 signals: in-flight resweep age gauge by path, last commit time by path (distinct from the watermark value), stored vs binary contract version, full-start reason (no_watermark, contract_version, keyed_cap), stand-down. Rollback: columns nullable, old binaries ignore them; a rollback to a pre-A binary or across a version bump must NULL the version columns (runbook step).
8. Deploy of 49da4cc774: plan stands with one change: a single pg_cancel_backend is not enough (the other pod retries within 30 s, the cancelled pod within ~60 s, each with a new snapshot; a full that starts before the build's second scan blocks the final wait again). Cancel whatever pg_stat_progress_create_index.current_locker_pid names during "waiting for old snapshots"; expect more than one cancel. Each cancel is accuracy-safe (one transaction, deferred rollback). Waiting for the running full opens no window. After the roll expect two back-to-back fulls, 3h33m-3h41m, no keyed tick; first commit ~T+1h47m-1h51m stamped T. Acceptable as a known cost; not a valid #10972 measurement window for anything waiting on the winners watermark: open the proof window at the first path="keyed" commit and do not roll again before then. After A+B steady lag still equals keyed backlog age (~55k rows at 6 keys/s ~ 2.5 h): hence priority:high on D.
